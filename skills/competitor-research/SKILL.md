# Competitor Research

The key decision is **who belongs in the comparison**. Find products and non-product alternatives that solve the same customer job before comparing features or business models.

## Workflow

1. **Define the job and scope.** Write who is trying to accomplish what, on which object, in which situation, and what successful outcome they seek. Separate this functional core from emotional or social benefits, input method, and interface mechanics. Record the target market, geography, language, and date. Done when another researcher could search for the same job without using the proposed product name.
2. **Search from the customer's problem and outcome.** Search the web, relevant app stores or marketplaces, and customer-language sources using problem, outcome, and alternative-action phrases. Vary wording and language where relevant; record the search surface and terms. Feature keywords may add candidates but cannot be the sole search strategy. Done when the query log covers multiple phrasings and each candidate has a source.
3. **Classify each candidate.** A **direct competitor** serves the same core job with a comparable solution; a **substitute** serves the same job through another method, including manual or offline behavior; a **similar mechanic** shares an input, interface, or sharing pattern but serves a different core job. Keep similar mechanics as analogies, not competitors. For every classification, cite what the candidate actually offers and explain the match or mismatch. Done when no candidate is classified on its name or marketing category alone.
4. **Check adjacent domains if direct matches are sparse.** Search for the same problem-solving pattern in neighboring categories. An adjacent example can suggest a mechanism or an experiment; it does **not** validate demand in the target market. If nothing is found, report search coverage rather than claiming the idea is unique. Done when the result distinguishes target-market evidence from analogy.
5. **Compare the relevant set.** Compare direct competitors and meaningful substitutes on the attributes that affect the customer's choice. Use [comparison dimensions](references/comparison-dimensions.md) selectively; skip dimensions whose evidence is unavailable or irrelevant. Record the source and observation date for material claims. Done when the comparison explains what a customer would choose instead and why.
6. **Synthesize the position and uncertainty.** State the closest alternative, the proposed difference, and whether that difference is observed, claimed, or untested. List exclusions, search coverage, unknowns, and the next customer or product check. Done when a reader can trace every competitive claim to evidence and cannot mistake a search miss for proof of absence.

## Report shape

- **Core job and scope:** customer, object, situation, outcome, market, date.
- **Search coverage:** surfaces, languages, representative queries, limits.
- **Candidate map:** candidate | direct / substitute / similar mechanic | evidence | classification reason.
- **Comparison:** only relevant dimensions, with citations and unknowns.
- **Position:** closest alternative, observed difference, untested hypothesis.
- **Next check:** the smallest test needed to resolve the consequential unknown.

Do not state “no competitors” or “demand is validated” from search results alone. State exactly what was checked and what remains unknown.
