# Idea Evaluation & Priority Scoring

## Core principles

- **Qualitative filtering comes first, quantitative scoring comes second.** The 4-axis check decides whether an idea is worth pursuing at all; ICE/RICE only orders the survivors. Don't mix the two — don't force an idea that clearly fails the 4-axis check into the scoring table.
- **Numbers are a basis for discussion, not a verdict.** Don't treat an ICE/RICE score as final. When scores land close together, resolve the tie with qualitative judgment (strategic importance, fit with current team capability). The score table exists to explain *why* this order, not to replace the decision.
- **Don't hide the evaluator's reasoning.** Attach a one-line justification to every axis/score. A number with no reasoning behind it is useless on a future re-review.
- **If there are fewer than 2 candidates, this skill isn't the right tool.** For a single idea being checked with "should we do this?", the 4-axis check alone is enough — skip the scoring step.

## Workflow

1. **List the candidates.** Enumerate everything up for evaluation. If a list already exists (backlog, idea doc), use that as the base.
2. **4-axis qualitative check (per candidate):**
   - **Market vs. problem**: How severe and how frequent is the problem? How large and how fast-growing is the market? (Need a strong yes on at least one of severity/growth.)
   - **Competitive edge**: Is there a clearly defined thing this does better than alternatives? (Price, marketing, team/talent, barriers to entry, or product advantage.)
   - **Scalability**: Is now the right time (external environment, regulation, technology inflection point)? Are there economies of scale or network effects?
   - **Founder drive**: Is there real energy behind actually pushing this through? (This axis is less a score, more an honest self-check.)
   - If 2 or more axes are clearly weak, drop the candidate here — don't carry it into scoring.
3. **ICE or RICE scoring (survivors only):**
   - Use **ICE** (Impact × Confidence × Ease, 1–5 each) when you need a fast call.
   - Use **RICE** (Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort) when effort varies a lot between candidates.
   - Scoring bands and formulas are in `references/scoring-templates.md`.
4. **Write up the results.** Build a table: candidate | one-line summary per axis | final score | rank. For close top contenders, add a short "pick X over Y because ___" note.
5. **Store it wherever fits.** For a one-off call, a table in the conversation is enough. For a recurring backlog, save it wherever priorities normally live (Notion, Linear, a vault, etc.).
